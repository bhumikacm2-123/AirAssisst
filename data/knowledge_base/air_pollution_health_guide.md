
# Air Pollution and Health Guide

## 1. What is Air Pollution?

Air pollution occurs when harmful substances such as
gases, smoke, dust and particulate matter enter the air.

## 2. Common Sources of Air Pollution

- Vehicle exhaust
- Industrial emissions
- Construction dust
- Burning of waste
- Road dust
- Fossil fuel combustion
- Agricultural residue burning

## 3. Health Effects of Air Pollution

Exposure to air pollution can affect different parts
of the human body.

### Respiratory Effects
- Coughing
- Wheezing
- Breathing discomfort
- Worsening of asthma symptoms

### Cardiovascular Effects
Long-term exposure to air pollution can increase
the risk of cardiovascular diseases.

### Sensitive Groups

The following groups may be more vulnerable:

- Children
- Older adults
- Pregnant women
- People with asthma
- People with existing heart or lung conditions

## 4. General Safety Precautions

- Check local AQI information regularly.
- Avoid prolonged outdoor exertion during high pollution.
- Follow local government health advisories.
- Keep indoor spaces as clean as practical.
- Avoid burning waste or other materials.
- Seek medical advice for persistent breathing problems.

## 5. Understanding Face Masks

A well-fitted respirator such as an N95 can help reduce
exposure to airborne particles.

A mask does not remove every air pollutant and does not
replace other protective measures.

## 6. Important Disclaimer

This document provides general educational information.
It does not diagnose, treat or prevent diseases.

People with health concerns should consult a qualified
healthcare professional.
