
# Indian Air Quality Index (AQI) Guide

## 1. What is AQI?

The Air Quality Index (AQI) is a numerical indicator
used to communicate the quality of outdoor air and
its potential health impacts.

India uses six AQI categories.

## 2. Indian AQI Categories

| Category | AQI Range | General Health Impact |
|---|---:|---|
| Good | 0-50 | Minimal impact |
| Satisfactory | 51-100 | Minor breathing discomfort to sensitive people |
| Moderate | 101-200 | Breathing discomfort for people with lung disease, children and older adults |
| Poor | 201-300 | Breathing discomfort on prolonged exposure |
| Very Poor | 301-400 | Increased risk of respiratory illness on prolonged exposure |
| Severe | 401-500 | Health effects may occur even in healthy people |

## 3. Pollutants Used in India's AQI

India's National AQI considers eight pollutants:

1. PM2.5 - Fine particulate matter
2. PM10 - Coarse particulate matter
3. NO2 - Nitrogen dioxide
4. SO2 - Sulphur dioxide
5. CO - Carbon monoxide
6. O3 - Ozone
7. NH3 - Ammonia
8. Pb - Lead

## 4. What is PM2.5?

PM2.5 refers to fine airborne particles with a
diameter of 2.5 micrometres or smaller.

These particles can penetrate deep into the lungs.

## 5. What is PM10?

PM10 refers to inhalable airborne particles with
a diameter of 10 micrometres or smaller.

## 6. General Precautions

- Check the local AQI before planning outdoor activities.
- Reduce prolonged or strenuous outdoor exertion when pollution is high.
- Sensitive individuals should follow official health advisories.
- Children and older adults may need additional protection.
- Seek medical advice if experiencing concerning respiratory symptoms.

## 7. Important Disclaimer

This document provides general educational information.
It is not a medical diagnosis or a substitute for
professional medical advice.

AQI categories and health guidance should be interpreted
using official CPCB and government health advisories.
