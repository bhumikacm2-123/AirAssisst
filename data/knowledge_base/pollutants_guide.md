
# Air Pollution Pollutants Guide

## 1. PM2.5

PM2.5 means fine particulate matter with a diameter
of 2.5 micrometres or smaller.

Sources:
- Vehicle emissions
- Industrial activities
- Burning of fuels
- Biomass burning

Health effects:
- Can penetrate deep into the lungs.
- Can worsen respiratory conditions.
- Long-term exposure can affect heart and lung health.

## 2. PM10

PM10 means particulate matter with a diameter
of 10 micrometres or smaller.

Sources:
- Road dust
- Construction activities
- Mining
- Industrial dust

Health effects:
- Can irritate the respiratory system.
- Can worsen asthma symptoms.

## 3. NO2 (Nitrogen Dioxide)

NO2 is a gaseous air pollutant mainly associated
with fuel combustion.

Sources:
- Motor vehicles
- Power plants
- Industrial combustion

Health effects:
- Can irritate airways.
- Can worsen respiratory diseases.

## 4. SO2 (Sulphur Dioxide)

SO2 is a gas commonly produced by burning
sulphur-containing fuels.

Sources:
- Coal combustion
- Industrial processes
- Some power plants

Health effects:
- Can cause respiratory irritation.
- Can worsen asthma symptoms.

## 5. CO (Carbon Monoxide)

CO is a colourless and odourless gas produced
by incomplete combustion.

Sources:
- Vehicle exhaust
- Generators
- Burning fuels indoors

Health effects:
- Reduces the blood's ability to carry oxygen.
- High exposure can cause serious poisoning.

## 6. O3 (Ground-Level Ozone)

Ground-level ozone forms through chemical reactions
between other pollutants in sunlight.

Health effects:
- Can cause coughing and throat irritation.
- Can worsen asthma.
- Can reduce lung function.

## 7. NH3 (Ammonia)

NH3 is a gas that can enter the atmosphere from
agricultural and industrial activities.

Sources:
- Fertilizers
- Livestock waste
- Industrial processes

Health effects:
- High concentrations can irritate the eyes
  and respiratory system.

## 8. Pb (Lead)

Lead is a toxic metal that can be present in
airborne particles from certain industrial sources.

Health effects:
- Can affect the nervous system.
- Children are particularly vulnerable to lead exposure.

## 9. Important Note

The health effects depend on pollutant concentration,
exposure duration and individual health conditions.

This document is for educational purposes only.
It is not a medical diagnosis.
