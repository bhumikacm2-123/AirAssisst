Understanding the Indian Air Quality Index (NAQI)

Official CPCB health classifications and color-coded risk bands

0 – 50 Good

Minimal health impact. Air quality is safe for all age groups and outdoor workouts.

51 – 100 Satisfactory

May cause minor breathing discomfort to sensitive individuals on prolonged exposure.

101 – 200 Moderate

Breathing discomfort to asthmatics, children, and elderly with lung or heart ailments.

201 – 300 Poor

Breathing discomfort to most people on prolonged exposure; wear N95 mask outdoors.

301 – 400 Very Poor

Can trigger respiratory illness on prolonged exposure; keep windows sealed and run HEPA filter.

401 – 500 Severe

Affects healthy people and seriously impacts vulnerable patients; avoid all outdoor exertion.

Public Health Knowledge Base

Clinical Understanding of Air Pollutants & Respiratory Protection

Scientific guidelines compiled from the World Health Organization (WHO), Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR), and AIIMS Delhi.

PM2.5 vs. PM10: Particle Dynamics

PM2.5: Combustible fine particles under 2.5 microns (1/30th the diameter of human hair). Because of their microscopic size, they bypass nasal cilia, settle deep into alveolar air sacs, and cross into capillaries, promoting coronary artery inflammation and thrombotic events.

PM10: Inhalable coarse dust particles under 10 microns (road dust, construction debris, pollen). Primarily deposit in upper airways, causing nasopharyngeal irritation, sinus headaches, and acute asthma exacerbations.

Toxic Gaseous Compounds

Nitrogen Dioxide (NO2): A pungent red-brown gas from vehicular combustion. Damages airway epithelial cells and exacerbates bronchitis in children.

Carbon Monoxide (CO): Binds to blood hemoglobin with 200x greater affinity than oxygen, forming carboxyhemoglobin ($COHb$) and starving myocardium and cerebral tissues of oxygen.

Ground-level Ozone (O3): Secondary photochemical pollutant formed on sunny afternoons. Triggers immediate chest tightness and reduces lung function.

Mask Selection: What Actually Protects

  • Cloth & Handkerchiefs: Filter less than 15-20% of PM2.5. Provide virtually zero sealing around the nose and jaw.
  • Surgical Masks: Designed for outward droplet containment, not particulate filtration. Over 60% of ambient air leaks through loose side gaps.
  • N95 / FFP2 / BIS 9473 Certified: Electrostatic meltblown nonwoven fabric capturing ≥95% of 0.3-micron particles. Mandatory for outdoor workers and two-wheeler commuters in AQI > 200.

Indoor Air Quality Management

  • True HEPA (H13 grade): Mechanically intercepts 99.97% of particulates without chemical byproducts.
  • CADR (Clean Air Delivery Rate): Ensure purifier CADR is at least 2/3 of the room's cubic foot volume for 4–5 air changes per hour (ACH).
  • Avoid Uncertified Ionizers: Electrostatic ionizers generate harmful secondary ozone ($O_3$) that damages respiratory membranes.
  • Damp Mopping: Clean indoor surfaces with damp microfiber cloths to eliminate settled lead and soot particles.

Vulnerable Population Action Matrix

Recommended behavioral adjustments based on Indian National AQI bands

AQI Band Healthy Population Asthma / COPD Patients Children & Elderly Outdoor Gig Workers
0 – 50 (Good) Ideal for all outdoor sports & running. Normal routine; safe for walks. Encourage outdoor play & recess. Standard shift without mask.
51 – 100 (Satisfactory) Safe for general activity. Observe for mild throat irritation. Safe for standard school activities. Stay hydrated during sunny hours.
101 – 200 (Moderate) Reduce continuous heavy sprint sessions. Keep rescue inhaler accessible; wear mask on roads. Limit prolonged aerobic sports practice. N95 mask recommended in dense traffic.
201 – 300 (Poor) Avoid early morning runs; shift workouts indoors. Remain indoors; avoid dusty roads; run purifier. Cancel outdoor physical training sessions. Mandatory N95 mask; frequent eye rinses.
301 – 400 (Very Poor) Keep windows closed; avoid outdoor exercise. Strictly stay indoors; run HEPA filtration 24/7. Keep children indoors; suspend school sports. Rotate shifts; rest in filtered spaces.
401 – 500+ (Severe) Health emergency; avoid all outdoor exposure. High risk of acute attack; keep physician on alert. Severe hazard; keep doors & windows sealed. Halt non-essential outdoor transit work.
